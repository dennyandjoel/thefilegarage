How this checker works

The process is designed to separate a real signature check from the visual copy created for easier reading and printing.

01Upload

Choose the PDF. If it is protected, enter its password when asked so the signature can be inspected.

02Validate

We check the signed bytes, document changes, certificate data, chain, revocation, timestamps, and the configured trust policy.

03Prepare a display copy

Only after the required checks pass, a separate PDF is produced with the validated appearance embedded at the signature location. A failed check never receives a green-valid copy.

Manual trust review

Most users do not need this step. It is useful only when a desktop PDF viewer cannot build trust for the issuing authority, even though the signature bytes and document integrity are valid.

  1. Open the original PDF in a desktop viewer that supports digital signatures.
  2. Click the signature panel (or right-click the signature) and open "Signature Properties", then click "Show Signer's Certificate".
  3. Open the signature panel or the signature properties for the relevant signature.
  4. View the signer’s certificate and inspect the certificate path or chain.
  5. Identify the highest certificate in the chain and confirm that the authority is one you trust.
  6. Open the "Trust" tab and click "Add to Trusted Certificates ... ".
  7. Tick "Use this certificate as a trusted root" and confirm with OK.
  8. Close the dialogs and click "Validate All" in the signature panel - the tick turns green on this computer.

This changes trust settings on that particular computer and viewer; it does not rewrite the original PDF. TheFileGarage's display copy is intended to keep the validated result visible when the file is previewed or printed elsewhere.
